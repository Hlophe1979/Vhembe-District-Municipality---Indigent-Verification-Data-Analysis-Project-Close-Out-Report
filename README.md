# Vhembe District Municipality: Indigent Verification Project Close-Out Report

## Project Overview

This project presents a three-year analysis of indigent application verification outcomes for Vhembe District Municipality, conducted by **Zimako Smart Business Solutions**.

The analysis covers records collected between March 2024 and October 2026 across the four local municipalities within Vhembe District. It examines application outcomes, applicant characteristics, verification flags, income categories, property ownership, business directorships, and geographic patterns.

The purpose of the analysis is to summarize the verification outcomes, identify data quality challenges and potential verification risks, and provide recommendations to improve indigent register management and municipal decision-making.

## Municipalities Covered

The analysis includes the following local municipalities:

- Collins Chabane Local Municipality
- Makhado Local Municipality
- Thulamela Local Municipality
- Musina Local Municipality

## Project Dataset

- **Total records analysed:** 170,091
- **Analysis period:** March 2024 – October 2026
- **Project:** Indigent Status Verification
- **Service provider:** Zimako Smart Business Solutions
- **District:** Vhembe District Municipality, Limpopo, South Africa

## Key Findings

### 1. Indigent Application Outcomes

Of the 170,091 records analysed:

- 99,340 applications were approved as indigent.
- 70,751 applications were declined.

These results provide an overview of the application outcomes recorded during the project period.

### 2. Applications by Municipality

Collins Chabane recorded the highest number of applications, followed by Makhado, Thulamela, and Musina.

| Municipality | Number of Records |
|---|---:|
| Collins Chabane | 77,124 |
| Makhado | 21,970 |
| Thulamela | 21,762 |
| Musina | 20,539 |

### 3. Deceased Applicant Analysis

The dataset recorded:

- 157,529 applicants as alive.
- 12,562 applicants as deceased.

Deceased-status records should be reviewed and managed in accordance with the municipality's applicable verification and register-update procedures.

### 4. Child-Headed Households

The analysis identified 36 child applicants classified under the child-headed household check. These records require appropriate review and handling in accordance with applicable municipal policies.

### 5. Multiple Indigent Accounts

A total of 52,365 applicants were flagged as having multiple indigent accounts.

Multiple-account flags may indicate duplicate registrations or other account-linkage issues. They should be investigated before conclusions are drawn about eligibility or possible misuse.

### 6. Income and Property Ownership

The income analysis found that:

- 48.88% of applicants were in the R0–R2,500 income category.
- 23.61% had unknown or unrecorded income information.
- 46.69% had no properties recorded.
- 13.55% had one property recorded.
- 3.46% had more than one property recorded.
- 36.30% had unknown or unrecorded property information.

Missing information limits the ability to make complete assessments of applicant circumstances and highlights the importance of improving data capture.

### 7. Registered Business Directorships

The analysis found that 94.43% of applicants were not linked to registered businesses in the recorded directorship data. Approximately 4.38% were linked to one company.

These indicators provide supporting information for verification review but should not, on their own, be treated as proof of eligibility or ineligibility.

### 8. Verification Risk Analysis

The close-out report classified application records as follows:

- 12.1% required urgent verification review.
- 30.5% required further assessment.
- 15.8% had no identified risk flags.
- 41.6% were declined as indigent.

Risk flags identify records that may require additional assessment. They are not, by themselves, proof of fraud or a final determination of eligibility.

### 9. Geographic Analysis

The geographic analysis identified Limpopo as the province associated with the majority of recorded applications, followed by Gauteng.

Municipality-level analysis showed different patterns in application volumes and outcomes. Collins Chabane recorded the highest volume of both approved and declined applications, while Makhado had a higher number of approvals than declines.

Geographic visualisations can help communicate these patterns and support planning, provided that location data is sufficiently complete and accurate.

## Data Quality Challenges

The analysis identified several issues affecting the completeness and reliability of the indigent applicant register:

- Duplicate records and multiple-account flags.
- Missing applicant information and incomplete fields.
- Inconsistent applicant identification information.
- Missing or ambiguous employer details.
- Incomplete residential address and geographic information.
- Differences in qualification criteria and data collection practices across municipalities.
- Variations in the use of verification and reporting systems.

These challenges may affect the accuracy of reporting, verification follow-ups, and resource allocation.

## Recommendations

### 1. Improve Data Validation

Introduce mandatory validation for critical fields, including applicant identification, address, and contact information. Conduct regular data quality audits to identify missing, inconsistent, and duplicate records.

### 2. Strengthen Duplicate Detection

Implement automated checks for duplicate applications, multiple accounts, identification mismatches, and possible household overlaps. Ensure that flagged records are reviewed before corrective action is taken.

### 3. Standardise Municipal Processes

Develop consistent data collection templates, standard operating procedures, and qualification criteria across the four local municipalities, subject to the applicable legal and policy framework.

### 4. Maintain a Central Digital Register

Maintain a secure, continuously updated district-wide register with access controls, audit trails, and change tracking. Establish clear responsibilities for maintaining accurate records.

### 5. Conduct Routine Re-Verification

Schedule periodic re-verification and targeted reviews of records with unresolved flags. Use appropriate and authorised data sources to support verification, subject to applicable privacy and data-protection requirements.

### 6. Improve Geographic Data Quality

Improve the completeness and standardisation of residential addresses and location fields. Validate geographic information before using it to generate maps or identify concentrations of applications.

### 7. Strengthen Staff Capacity

Provide regular training on verification systems, data management, reporting procedures, and data quality controls. Assign responsibility for register accuracy at municipal level.

### 8. Monitor Outcomes Regularly

Produce periodic reports on approval and decline trends, duplicate-account flags, missing information, and verification outcomes. Use consistent definitions and measures to enable comparisons over time.

The findings reflect the data and classifications presented in the project close-out report.
